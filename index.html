Engineering notes, assumptions and pilot-testing requirements

What this is

A proposed integrated system for a southern Bangladesh pond: ducks, fish and floating vegetable rafts share one pond, while a pumped loop of screening, settling, biological filtration and — if selected after testing — UV treatment protects water quality. This page is an explanatory model, not a construction drawing, and contains no design dimensions, stocking densities, flow rates, pump sizes, dosing amounts or income figures.

The shared pond

  • Ducks use a fenced, designated pond-access area only. A duck-exclusion barrier keeps them out of the raft zone and the intake.
  • Droppings and spilled feed are a limited, managed organic input to the pond — not a disposal route.
  • Most litter, bedding and manure is collected and composted on the bank, separated from the water.
  • Fish use the open water; floating rafts shade part of the surface and provide structure.

Water path (filtered loop)

  1. Screened intake in the pond.
  2. Intake pump and valve.
  3. Settling / mechanical chamber: settleable solids leave as sludge.
  4. Aerated biofilter: nitrifying bacteria convert ammonia → nitrite → nitrate.
  5. Optional UV barrier, only if the design is validated for the intended duty.
  6. Enclosed root channels beneath the floating rafts.
  7. Return to the pond.

Sludge and manure follow brown routes to a sludge container and compost — never into the biofilter or root channels.

Quicklime (calcium oxide) — safety

  • CaO reacts vigorously with water and raises pH; at higher pH a larger fraction of total ammonia is toxic NH₃.
  • CaO must not enter the active pond, filters, operating biofilter, root channels or return pipe.
  • If testing shows low alkalinity, a fisheries specialist may consider agricultural limestone (calcium carbonate) as a controlled correction — never an automatic dose.
  • The CaO illustration in the safety panel shows a separate, drained pond during pre-stocking preparation only; it is not connected to the operating system.

Ammonia and pH model

The calculator uses the freshwater equilibrium estimate pKa = 0.09018 + 2729.92 / (273.2 + T) and NH₃ fraction = 1 / (1 + 10^(pKa − pH)). It is an educational estimate based on US EPA equilibrium data, not a farm operating threshold and not a dosing tool.

Assumptions and values to be established in pilot testing

  • Pond size and depth, freeboard, and flood-survival height.
  • Duck and fish numbers, feed quantity and composition, and the acceptable organic loading to the pond.
  • Pump duty, pipe sizes, filter media volume and aeration capacity.
  • Whether UV is required, and its validated dose and contact time.
  • Raft size, buoyancy, root-channel geometry and vegetable species.
  • All water-quality limits used for operational decisions, set with local fisheries expertise and testing.

Flood mode

Flood mode illustrates rafts rising with water and the bank staying serviceable. The system is flood-adaptive, not floodproof: fixed pipes, pumps and the biofilter need a protective plan, and pipes do not move with the rafts in this illustration.

Pilot-testing checklist (before construction)

  • Baseline pond and source-water chemistry, including alkalinity and ammonia.
  • Water balance and flood levels across seasons.
  • Solids capture and sludge removal frequency.
  • Biofilter start-up and ammonia/nitrite response.
  • Duck health, welfare and exclusion-barrier performance.
  • Food-safety plan for vegetables grown over pond water.

The nitrogen cycle in the biofilter

Ammonia NH₃ / NH₄⁺→Nitrite NO₂⁻→Nitrate NO₃⁻

Fish and ducks release ammonia. Helpful bacteria living on the inert filter media convert ammonia into nitrite and then into nitrate. Plants can take up some of the nitrate as a nutrient.

The biofilter needs oxygen and continuous water movement. Never add disinfectants or quicklime to the operating biofilter — it would harm the bacteria the system depends on.

Conversion is an established biological process. Filter media volume and carrying capacity must be established during pilot testing.

Why quicklime is not used in the active system

Calcium oxide (quicklime) reacts with water and raises pH rapidly. At higher pH, a larger share of total ammonia exists as toxic NH₃. It is not a routine ammonia-removal treatment.

Do not add quicklime to the occupied duck–fish pond, the filters, the operating biofilter, the root channels or the return pipe.

Ammonia is managed by balanced feed input, controlled duck and fish loading, solids separation, biological filtration, aeration and water-quality monitoring.

If testing confirms low alkalinity, a fisheries specialist may consider agricultural limestone (calcium carbonate) for controlled correction — never an automatic dose.

This is a separate maintenance procedure requiring fisheries expertise. It is not connected to the operating farm.
